Design summary (what, why, how)
- Target framework: a high-entropy Prussian blue analog of the form Ax(Ni, Co, Mn, Zn)[Fe, Co(CN)6]y·nH2O.
- Recommended example composition (tunable): K0.3(Ni0.35Co0.20Mn0.25Zn0.20)[Fe0.75Co0.25(CN)6]0.85·2.5H2O grown directly on Ni foam or carbon cloth.
- Rationale in one line: Ni supplies the primary urea-oxidation active sites, Co and Fe co-tune the oxyhydroxide chemistry and electronic coupling through the CN bridges, Mn broadens redox buffering and defect chemistry, and Zn is a benign, low-cost stabilizer that enhances corrosion resistance and structural integrity while helping create controlled porosity.

Why a high-entropy PBA for UOR in alkaline media
- Activity: PBAs provide robust, CN-bridged M–N≡C–M′ networks with fast OH−/urea transport through intrinsic porosity and interstitial water; mixing multiple metals adjusts adsorption energetics (OH−, NH2, CO*, CO3 2−) at the reconstructed oxyhydroxide surface formed in operando.
- Stability: High configurational entropy suppresses phase segregation and coarsening during potential cycling; strong M′–C≡N–M bonds (especially with CoIII/FeIII on the [M′(CN)6] site) mitigate dissolution.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: One-pot, room-temperature aqueous co-precipitation from earth-abundant salts (Ni, Co, Mn, Zn, Fe) and commercial K3[Fe(CN)6]/K3[Co(CN)6]; no noble metals or exotic precursors needed.
- Environmental friendliness: Avoids Ru/Ir/Pt/Pd (scarce), Cr(VI)/V (higher toxicity concerns), and complex Mo/W cyanometallates (less accessible, higher synthesis footprint). Uses stable hexacyanometallate salts rather than free cyanide.

Detailed element-by-element rationale

1) Nickel (Ni)
- Catalytic activity: The key UOR-active phase is γ-NiOOH formed from Ni2+ under anodic bias in alkaline media. NiOOH provides sites for dehydrogenation and C–N/C–H bond activation in urea, lowering onset potential and improving kinetics. Ni–O–Fe/Co ensembles further accelerate the rate-determining dehydrogenation/adsorption steps.
- Structural/electronic role: As the N-coordinated M2+ node, Ni couples through CN bridges to low-spin CoIII/FeIII on the [M′(CN)6] site, increasing covalency and electronic conductivity. Mixed neighbors tune the Ni eg occupancy and promote formation of high-valent Ni3+/Ni4+ under bias.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: Embedding Ni in a high-entropy lattice reduces localized dissolution and suppresses particle growth; strong CN bridges and nearby CoIII/FeIII stiffen the framework during redox cycling.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: Readily sourced as Ni(NO3)2 or NiSO4; co-precipitates cleanly with hexacyanometallates. Ni is inexpensive at scale.
- Environmental impact: No unusual hazards beyond standard Ni handling; fully compatible with aqueous, low-temperature synthesis.

2) Cobalt (Co)
- Catalytic activity: On the N-site as Co2+, Co enhances OH− adsorption and charge-transfer to Ni, lowering the energy barrier for urea dehydrogenation. On the [M′(CN)6] site as low-spin CoIII, it stiffens CN bridges and improves electronic coupling; Co also contributes to bifunctional UOR/OER activity, useful under mixed-analyte or high-current conditions.
- Structural/electronic role: [Co(CN)6]3− is a strong-field, π-accepting unit that increases the covalency of M–CN–M′ and raises the framework’s electronic conductivity. Co2+ on the M site introduces additional redox flexibility (Co2+/3+) and local lattice distortion that create active defects without destabilizing the phase when moderated by Zn and Fe.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: CoIII on the cyanide node tightens the framework and helps resist hydrolysis; combined Ni–Co oxyhydroxides formed in situ are known to be durable in alkaline electrolytes.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: Co salts and K3[Co(CN)6] are common laboratory reagents; co-precipitation is straightforward. Cost is moderate and far below noble metals.
- Environmental note: Co mining has sustainability considerations; the proposed composition limits Co to a minority fraction and offers a low-Co variant by increasing [Fe(CN)6] if desired.

3) Iron (Fe)
- Catalytic activity: Fe incorporation synergistically accelerates Ni-based UOR by modifying the oxygen-intermediate binding and facilitating lattice-oxygen participation at the reconstructed oxyhydroxide surface. Fe-containing PBAs exhibit intervalence charge transfer (FeII/FeIII) that enhances electronic transport to active Ni sites.
- Structural/electronic role: [Fe(CN)6]4−/3− is the archetypal cyanometallate for PBAs, giving excellent framework-forming ability. Mixed FeII/FeIII enables charge compensation and controlled [M′(CN)6] vacancies, which increase porosity and hydroxide/urea accessibility while preserving long-range order.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: Strong Fe–C≡N bonds and the ability to accommodate vacancies/water molecules help dissipate strain during the Ni2+→NiOOH transition, enhancing cycle life.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: K4[Fe(CN)6] and K3[Fe(CN)6] are inexpensive bulk chemicals, easy to handle in water; using these avoids free cyanide.
- Environmental friendliness: Iron is abundant and benign; hexacyanoferrates are stable complexes with very low free CN− release under neutral/alkaline conditions.

4) Manganese (Mn)
- Catalytic activity: Mn provides additional redox couples (Mn2+/3+/4+) that buffer potential-induced structural changes and facilitate OH− activation, which can accelerate urea dehydrogenation at neighboring Ni/Co sites. Mn helps create oxygen vacancies/defect sites after mild electrochemical activation, increasing intrinsic activity.
- Structural/electronic role: High-spin Mn2+ on the N site increases local lattice flexibility, promoting microstrain that boosts the density of catalytically relevant defects without compromising the robust CN-bridged network. It also broadens the configurational-entropy stabilization.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: In a multi-cation lattice, Mn’s propensity for disproportionation/dissolution is reduced; CoIII/FeIII cyanide nodes and Zn2+ co-stabilize the octahedral environment, limiting Mn loss during extended operation.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: Mn salts are cheap and mix well in standard PBA co-precipitations; no special handling required.
- Environmental note: Mn is relatively benign among transition metals; inclusion at moderate fraction is considered environmentally acceptable.

5) Zinc (Zn) — the “benign, stable-valence” optimizer
- Catalytic activity: Zn2+ is largely redox-inactive under UOR conditions, but it enhances apparent activity by engineering the microstructure. Controlled Zn content can induce mild, self-limiting leaching during initial activation, creating mesoporosity and exposing more Ni/Co/Fe sites; it also adjusts local electron density around active Ni, facilitating NiOOH formation.
- Structural/electronic role: d10 Zn2+ is a strong lattice stabilizer that reduces Jahn–Teller distortions and helps maintain long-range order while increasing water content and hydrophilicity in the A- and vacancy channels. It improves corrosion resistance by decreasing the driving force for aggressive oxidative dissolution of other cations.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: Zn’s presence increases the formation energy for cation migration and reduces crack formation under cycling. Its environmentally benign nature makes it ideal as the “fifth element” to balance high activity with green, durable design.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: Extremely low cost and easy aqueous processing; no complex cyanide chemistry required since Zn occupies the N site as a simple divalent cation.
- Environmental friendliness: Zinc is one of the most benign transition metals; its use addresses the brief’s call for a stable-valence, green element to optimize performance.

Why not other common choices
- Cu: Cu2+ can improve conductivity but introduces strong Jahn–Teller distortion (d9) and has higher leaching risk in alkaline anodic environments, compromising long-term stability and corrosion resistance.
- Cr or V: Even if present in low-toxicity cyanide complexes, regulatory and environmental concerns (especially for Cr(VI) and vanadate species) argue against their inclusion for a green catalyst.
- Mo/W: Cyanometallate chemistry is less standard (octacyanometallates, more complex synthesis), increasing cost and lowering synthetic simplicity.
- Ru/Ir/Pt/Pd: Excellent activity but fail cost, scarcity, and environmental criteria.

Structural features and how each metal supports them
- Mixed [Fe,Co(CN)6] node: Fe provides redox conduction and vacancy tolerance; CoIII strengthens CN bonds and framework rigidity—together they balance conductivity and structural integrity.
- Multi-cation N site (Ni/Co/Mn/Zn): Ni supplies the UOR engine; Co tunes OH−/urea adsorption and kinetics; Mn introduces controlled lattice flexibility and redox buffering; Zn stabilizes the framework and mitigates corrosion. The statistical distribution of these cations produces high configurational entropy that resists phase separation.
- Vacancies and interstitials: Slight [M′(CN)6] deficiency (y < 1) and K+/Na+ A-site occupancy create ion/water channels that speed mass transport; these vacancies are stabilized by Fe/Co on the C site and Zn/Mn on the N site.
- Operando surface reconstruction: Under UOR bias, the outer layers convert to a mixed Ni–Fe–Co oxyhydroxide rich in active NiOOH sites, while the PBA core and CN bridges maintain a conductive, mechanically supportive scaffold—Mn and Zn help this core–shell stability.

Synthesis and processing notes (ease, safety, scalability)
- Precursors: Aqueous Ni(NO3)2, Co(NO3)2, Mn(NO3)2, Zn(NO3)2 (or sulfates/chlorides), K3[Fe(CN)6], K3[Co(CN)6], K2CO3/KOH for pH control, optional trisodium citrate as a green chelating/shape-control agent.
- Method: Room-temperature co-precipitation with slow addition of hexacyanometallate solution into the mixed M2+ solution under vigorous stirring; pH near neutral to avoid hydroxide precipitation; age, wash, and dry. Grow directly on Ni foam/carbon cloth to eliminate polymer binders and improve corrosion resistance.
- Vacancy control: Use a slight deficiency of [Fe,Co(CN)6] relative to M2+ to target y ≈ 0.8–0.9; mild 80–120 C drying tunes n(H2O) for porosity and conductivity balance.
- Safety/environment: Use preformed hexacyanometallates to avoid free CN−; aqueous, solvent-free processing; low-temperature steps minimize energy input and environmental footprint.

Cost and sustainability snapshot
- All five metals (Ni, Co, Fe, Mn, Zn) are commodity-scale, with Fe/Mn/Zn being especially low-cost. Total catalyst materials cost is orders of magnitude below noble-metal analogs.
- Minimal energy input and water-based synthesis reduce embodied energy and emissions.
- The design limits cobalt use and avoids Cr/V, aligning with environmental and regulatory goals.

Corrosion resistance and durability features
- Strong [Co/Fe(CN)6] nodes and Zn-rich local environments resist dissolution and cracking during redox cycling.
- High-entropy mixing suppresses phase segregation that otherwise leads to catalyst deactivation.
- Binder-free growth on corrosion-resistant supports (Ni foam, carbon cloth) minimizes interfacial failure; optional thin carbon overcoats or ALD-grown oxide nanolayers can be added if extreme durability is required without compromising green credentials.

Performance expectations and tuning levers
- Expect significantly lowered UOR onset potential and Tafel slope versus single-metal PBAs or pure Ni catalysts, with stable operation over tens to hundreds of hours in 1.0 M KOH + 0.33–1.0 M urea.
- Tuning levers:
  - Increase Co fraction on the [M′(CN)6] site to boost conductivity and rigidity if stability is limiting.
  - Increase Fe fraction and [M′(CN)6] vacancies to raise porosity and active-site exposure if kinetics are limiting.
  - Adjust Zn content to balance initial porosity creation with long-term corrosion resistance.
  - Optimize particle size (50–200 nm nanocubes/hollow structures) for diffusion and ECSA.

Bottom line
- The proposed five-metal high-entropy PBA—Ax(Ni, Co, Mn, Zn)[Fe, Co(CN)6]y·nH2O—meets the brief across catalytic activity, durability, low cost, simplicity of synthesis, environmental friendliness, and corrosion resistance. Each element’s role is deliberately chosen: Ni (primary UOR engine), Co (kinetic accelerator and framework stiffener), Fe (electronic conductor and vacancy manager), Mn (redox buffer and defect promoter), and Zn (benign stabilizer and porosity modulator). This combination leverages well-established cyanometallate chemistry for easy, green fabrication and high practical performance.
