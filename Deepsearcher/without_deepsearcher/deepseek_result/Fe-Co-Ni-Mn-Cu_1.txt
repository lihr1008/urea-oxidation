## **Design Rationale for High-Entropy Prussian Blue Analogue (HE-PBA) Catalyst for Urea Oxidation Reaction (UOR)**  

**Target application:** Alkaline urea oxidation reaction (UOR: CO(NH₂)₂ + 6OH⁻ → N₂ + 5H₂O + CO₂ + 6e⁻)  
**Material:** High-entropy Prussian blue analogue (HE-PBA) with general formula:  
\[
\text{A}_x[\text{M}_1\text{M}_2\text{M}_3\text{M}_4\text{M}_5](\text{CN})_6·n\text{H}_2\text{O}
\]  
Where M₁–M₅ are transition metals in near-equimolar ratio occupying the same metal–N coordination site (or metal–C site in the PB framework).  

**Selected five transition metals (proposed combination):**  
**Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Mn**  

---

### **1. Overall Multi-Criteria Rationale**
High-entropy PBAs leverage configurational entropy to stabilize multiple metals in a cyanide-bridged framework, which enhances structural robustness, modifies electronic environments, and creates multiple active sites for urea adsorption and oxidation. For UOR in alkaline media, key catalytic factors include:  
- **Modulated d-band centers** for optimized urea adsorption strength.  
- **Redox flexibility** to facilitate oxidation steps (involving NH₃ dehydrogenation and CO/N–N bond cleavage).  
- **Corrosion resistance** to withstand alkaline oxidative potentials.  
- **Ionic conductivity & stability** for long-term cycling.  

Each metal is chosen based on complementary roles to fulfill **activity, stability, cost, and environmental safety**.

---

### **2. Individual Metal Justifications**  

#### **2.1 Iron (Fe)**  
- **Catalytic role:** Fe in PBAs gives strong cyanide bonding that stabilizes the cubic framework; Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ redox couple can participate in electron transfer steps in UOR. Fe incorporation improves electrical conductivity in PBA structure and enhances synergistic interactions with neighboring metals via electronic modulation.  
- **Stability:** Fe-based PBAs are extremely stable in alkaline conditions compared to acid; cyanide linkage protects against leaching.  
- **Cost & environment:** Earth-abundant, low-cost, non-toxic as hexacyanoferrate (used in food/pharmaceutical grades).  
- **Structural role:** Provides firm structural backbone via strong Fe–N/O bonds, minimizing lattice distortion when combined with other metals.  

#### **2.2 Cobalt (Co)**  
- **Catalytic role:** Co is a well-known high-activity center for UOR (Co³⁺/Co⁴⁺ redox in alkaline media promotes dehydrogenation of urea intermediates). Co sites facilitate C–N bond cleavage in urea. In PBAs, CoN₆ octahedra are redox-active at potentials near UOR (~0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl in 1 M NaOH).  
- **Stability:** Co–CN–Fe bridges are robust; cobalt hexacyanoferrates show good electrochemical stability.  
- **Cost:** More expensive than Fe/Ni but still reasonably abundant; used in modest proportion here (20 % atomic).  
- **Environmental:** Less toxic than Cr or Cd; manageable in PBA form.  

#### **2.3 Nickel (Ni)**  
- **Catalytic role:** Ni(OH)₂/NiOOH surface on PBAs during alkaline conditioning provides the main UOR activity. Ni³⁺ is a strong oxidant for urea oxidation. Ni inclusion ensures high catalytic current density. Ni and Co synergy in PBAs reduces overpotential for UOR.  
- **Stability:** Ni–C bonds in PBA resist corrosion; NiOOH surface layer self-passivating.  
- **Cost & environment:** Low cost, abundant, environmentally benign.  
- **Structural role:** Ni²⁺ (r = 0.69 Å) fits well in PBA lattice, moderate Jahn–Teller effect manageable in high-entropy mixing.  

#### **2.4 Copper (Cu)**  
- **Catalytic role:** Cu introduces redox versatility (Cu²⁺/Cu³⁺ in alkaline conditions) assisting in potential-determining steps. Cu can promote N–N coupling or N₂ release in UOR mechanism. Also improves electrical conductivity of the catalyst film.  
- **Stability:** Cu–cyanide bonds stable in alkaline media; Cu may promote faster kinetics.  
- **Cost & environment:** Cheap, abundant, less toxic than precious metals. Potential leaching in extreme pH mitigated by cyanide coordination.  
- **Structural role:** Cu²⁺ Jahn–Teller distortion can be suppressed by entropy stabilization, possibly creating strained active sites beneficial for catalysis.  

#### **2.5 Manganese (Mn)**  
- **Catalytic role:** Mn is introduced as the **“metal with stable valence states and environmentally benign character”**. Mn³⁺/Mn²⁺ redox can act as electron reservoir, modulating the oxidation states of neighboring Co/Ni to more active states. Mn also stabilizes oxygen intermediates during UOR. Mn inclusion reduces reliance on expensive or toxic metals (e.g., avoids Pt, Pd, Cr, Cd, etc.).  
- **Stability:** Mn in PBA framework is stable in moderate alkaline conditions; high-spin Mn–N bonds provide lattice flexibility without collapse.  
- **Cost & environment:** Very low cost, abundant, non-toxic, environmentally friendly (Mn compounds used in fertilizers).  
- **Structural role:** Mn’s ionic radius (Mn²⁺ 0.83 Å) close to other selected metals → minimal lattice strain; entropy stabilization prevents phase segregation.  

---

### **3. Synergistic Benefits of the Quinary System (Fe–Co–Ni–Cu–Mn)**  
- **Electronic synergy:** The mixed d-electron configuration adjusts the Fermi level and d-band center for optimal urea binding energy, avoiding too strong/too weak adsorption.  
- **Redox synergy:** Multiple accessible oxidation states within a narrow potential window provide stepwise electron transfer for the 6-electron UOR process.  
- **Entropic stabilization:** High configurational entropy inhibits detrimental phase separation during cycling, enhancing durability.  
- **Cost balance:** All five metals are abundant (low supply risk); Mn further lowers environmental footprint vs. using precious/dangerous metals.  
- **Alkaline compatibility:** Cyanide-coordination environment in PBAs prevents hydroxide-induced metal leaching for Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Mn at moderate alkalinity. Corrosion resistance superior to pure metal hydroxides.  

---

### **4. Avoided Metals & Reasons**  
- **Cr, Cd, Pb:** Toxic, environmentally hazardous.  
- **Pt, Pd, Ir:** High cost, scarce, overkill for urea oxidation.  
- **Zn:** Tendency to dissolve in strong alkali despite cyanide coordination; less active for UOR.  
- **Mo, W:** Less compatible with PBA cyanide crystal field; may form competing oxide phases during synthesis.  
- **Ag:** Expensive and can form AgCN precipitate during synthesis; not stable under high anodic potentials in alkali.  

---

### **5. Synthetic Feasibility**  
HE-PBAs can be synthesized via one-pot co-precipitation from aqueous solutions of metal salts and hexacyanoferrate (or mixed hexacyanometallates) at room temperature. High-entropy mixing is promoted by rapid precipitation kinetics and high solubility of cyanide complexes. The process is:  
- **Simple & scalable** → low production cost.  
- **Water-based** → environmentally friendly synthesis.  
- **No high-temperature annealing required** → retains open porous framework for UOR mass transport.  

---

### **6. Structural Features Relevant to UOR**  
1. **Porous framework** → high surface area, efficient diffusion of urea/OH⁻ to active sites.  
2. **Mixed metals in N-coordinated sites** → tunable Lewis acidity for urea adsorption.  
3. **Flexible valence states** → redox buffering effect during UOR cycling.  
4. **Cubic lattice stability** → minimal volume change during charge transfer, ensuring mechanical integrity.  

---

### **Conclusion**  
The proposed **Fe–Co–Ni–Cu–Mn** high-entropy PBA balances high UOR activity (from Co, Ni, Cu), structural stability (Fe, Mn, entropy), low cost (all abundant), eco-friendliness (especially Mn replacing toxic metals), and corrosion resistance (cyanide framework in alkali). Mn introduction specifically mitigates environmental concerns while contributing to redox cooperativity, making the catalyst suitable for sustainable energy applications such as urea-based fuel cells or wastewater-to-energy systems.
