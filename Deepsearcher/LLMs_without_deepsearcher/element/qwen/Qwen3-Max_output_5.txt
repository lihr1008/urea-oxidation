Designing a high-entropy Prussian blue analog (HE-PBA) catalyst for the urea oxidation reaction (UOR) in alkaline media requires careful selection of five transition metals that synergistically enhance catalytic activity, structural stability, and practical viability. Below is a detailed rationale for selecting **Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, and Cu** as the constituent transition metals in the HE-PBA framework.

---

### **1. General Framework: Prussian Blue Analogs (PBAs)**
Prussian blue analogs have the general formula \( A_xM[M'(CN)_6]_y·nH_2O \), where \( M \) and \( M' \) are transition metals occupying octahedral sites coordinated to nitrogen and carbon, respectively. PBAs offer:
- An open, face-centered cubic framework facilitating ion/electron transport.
- Tunable redox chemistry via metal center selection.
- Structural robustness under alkaline conditions.
- Scalable aqueous synthesis at ambient conditions.

High-entropy PBAs incorporate **five or more principal elements** in near-equimolar ratios, leveraging entropy stabilization to suppress phase segregation and enhance durability.

---

### **2. Selection Criteria Overview**
Each metal was chosen based on:
- **Catalytic activity for UOR**: Ability to form high-valent oxyhydroxide species (*e.g.*, Ni³⁺/⁴⁺, Co³⁺/⁴⁺) that facilitate urea dehydrogenation.
- **Structural stability**: Compatibility with PBA lattice; resistance to dissolution or Jahn–Teller distortion.
- **Cost & abundance**: Earth-abundant, low-cost elements.
- **Environmental benignity**: Low toxicity, regulatory acceptance.
- **Synthetic compatibility**: Solubility in aqueous precursors, similar ionic radii for solid solution formation.

---

### **3. Rationale for Each Metal**

#### **(a) Nickel (Ni) – Primary UOR Active Site**
- **Catalytic Role**: Ni²⁺ oxidizes to Ni³⁺/⁴⁺ oxyhydroxides (*e.g.*, NiOOH) in alkali, which are *the most active known sites* for UOR via adsorption and dehydrogenation of urea.
- **Stability**: Forms stable [Ni(CN)₆]⁴⁻ complexes; integrates well into PBA lattices.
- **Cost & Environment**: Abundant (~$20/kg), non-toxic, widely used in batteries.
- **Structural Fit**: Ionic radius (Ni²⁺ HS: 0.69 Å) matches PBA octahedral sites.

> **Contribution**: Provides the core UOR activity; essential for lowering overpotential.

#### **(b) Cobalt (Co) – Synergistic Redox Mediator**
- **Catalytic Role**: Co³⁺/⁴⁺ species enhance charge transfer kinetics and stabilize Ni³⁺ states via electronic modulation. Co also directly participates in urea adsorption.
- **Stability**: Co³⁺ is stable in alkaline media; [Co(CN)₆]³⁻ is robust.
- **Cost & Environment**: Moderately priced (~$30/kg), less toxic than Cr or V.
- **Structural Fit**: Co²⁺ (0.745 Å) and Co³⁺ (0.61 Å) fit PBA geometry without distortion.

> **Contribution**: Boosts intrinsic activity through electronic coupling with Ni; improves conductivity.

#### **(c) Iron (Fe) – Structural Stabilizer & Cost Reducer**
- **Catalytic Role**: Fe³⁺ contributes modest UOR activity but primarily modulates electronic structure of neighboring Ni/Co sites, enhancing OH⁻ adsorption.
- **Stability**: Fe³⁺ in [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ forms the classic Prussian blue backbone—highly stable in alkali.
- **Cost & Environment**: Extremely cheap (<$1/kg), non-toxic, abundant.
- **Structural Fit**: Ideal ionic radius (Fe³⁺: 0.645 Å); promotes long-range order.

> **Contribution**: Anchors the PBA framework; reduces overall cost while improving mechanical integrity.

#### **(d) Manganese (Mn) – Eco-Friendly Promoter with Multi-Valency**
- **Catalytic Role**: Mn²⁺/³⁺/⁴⁺ redox couples facilitate OER/UOR bifunctionality and promote *in situ* formation of active (oxy)hydroxide phases. Mn enhances surface hydrophilicity for urea access.
- **Stability**: Mn²⁺ integrates into PBAs as [Mn(CN)₆]⁴⁻; though prone to mild dissolution, high-entropy effect mitigates this.
- **Cost & Environment**: Very low cost (~$2/kg), essential nutrient, environmentally benign.
- **Structural Fit**: Mn²⁺ (0.83 Å) is slightly large but accommodated in disordered HE-PBA lattices.

> **Contribution**: Introduces flexible redox chemistry; aligns with green chemistry principles.

#### **(e) Copper (Cu) – Electronic Modulator & Corrosion Resistor**
- **Catalytic Role**: Cu⁺/²⁺ modulates d-band center of adjacent metals, optimizing urea adsorption energy. Cu also suppresses competing oxygen evolution reaction (OER), improving UOR selectivity.
- **Stability**: Cu²⁺ forms stable [Cu(CN)₄]²⁻ but adapts to PBA via partial occupancy; notable corrosion resistance in alkali.
- **Cost & Environment**: Low cost (~$8/kg), recyclable, low ecotoxicity.
- **Structural Fit**: Cu²⁺ (0.73 Å) fits well; Jahn–Teller distortion is mitigated by entropy-driven lattice disorder.

> **Contribution**: Enhances selectivity toward UOR over OER; improves long-term corrosion resistance.

---

### **4. High-Entropy Synergy**
- **Entropy Stabilization**: Configurational entropy (\( \Delta S_{\text{config}} = R \ln 5 \approx 1.61R \)) suppresses phase separation, yielding a single-phase PBA even with dissimilar metals.
- **Lattice Strain & Defects**: Atomic-level disorder creates strained bonds and unsaturated sites, boosting intrinsic activity.
- **Multi-Metal Redox Network**: Enables rapid electron hopping (Fe²⁺/³⁺ ↔ Co²⁺/³⁺ ↔ Ni²⁺/³⁺ ↔ Mn²⁺/³⁺ ↔ Cu⁺/²⁺), accelerating kinetics.
- **Self-Reconstruction Resistance**: High mixing enthalpy and sluggish diffusion kinetics inhibit leaching during UOR.

---

### **5. Practical Advantages**
- **Synthesis**: All metal precursors (nitrates or chlorides) are water-soluble; co-precipitation at room temperature yields homogeneous HE-PBA.
- **Scalability**: No noble metals; total raw material cost < $10/kg.
- **Environmental Profile**: All metals are EPA-approved for industrial use; no Cr, V, or Cd.
- **Performance Metrics**: Expected UOR onset potential < 1.32 V vs. RHE, current density > 100 mA/cm² at 1.5 V, stability > 100 h in 1 M KOH + 0.33 M urea.

---

### **6. Why Avoid Other Metals?**
- **Zn**: Lacks redox activity in alkali; volatile during operation.
- **Cr/V**: Toxic, regulated, expensive.
- **Mo/W**: High cost, complex synthesis, poor PBA compatibility.
- **Ti/Zr**: Inert in UOR, incompatible cyanide coordination.

---

### **Conclusion**
The **Fe–Co–Ni–Mn–Cu** high-entropy PBA represents an optimal balance:
- **Ni** and **Co** drive UOR activity,
- **Fe** ensures structural and economic viability,
- **Mn** adds eco-friendly redox flexibility,
- **Cu** fine-tunes selectivity and durability.

This composition leverages high-entropy effects to transcend the limitations of single- or dual-metal PBAs, delivering a scalable, sustainable, and high-performance UOR electrocatalyst for applications such as energy-saving hydrogen production and urea-rich wastewater remediation.
