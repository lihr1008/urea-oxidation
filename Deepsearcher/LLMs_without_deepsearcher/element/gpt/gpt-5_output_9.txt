Recommended high-entropy Prussian blue analog (HE-PBA): (Ni,Co,Mn,Zn)–Fe PBA
A practical, low-cost, and environmentally considerate five-metal HE-PBA for urea oxidation in alkaline media can be formulated as:
(Ni0.30Co0.20Mn0.20Zn0.30)3[Fe(CN)6]2·nH2O
- M site (N-coordinated): high-entropy mixture of Ni2+, Co2+, Mn2+/3+, Zn2+
- M′ site (C-coordinated): Fe3+/Fe2+ as [Fe(CN)6]3−/4− nodes
This places four metals on the N-coordinated sublattice and Fe on the cyanometallate sublattice, yielding five transition metals overall. Fe(CN)6 is chosen as the cyanometallate because it is abundant, benign, and synthetically robust, enabling straightforward aqueous co-precipitation. The composition uses a modest Co fraction to limit cost/toxicity, and intentionally includes Zn as a benign, stable-valence element to improve stability and corrosion resistance without greatly raising cost.

Why this HE-PBA for UOR in alkaline media
- Activity: Ni-centered oxyhydroxide (NiOOH/NiIII–IV) is the canonical active phase for UOR; Fe and Co are well-known promoters that lower the Ni2+/Ni3+ activation potential and facilitate charge transfer; Mn tunes the redox landscape and oxygen ligand participation; Zn modulates local geometry and defect chemistry while maintaining lattice integrity. The HE environment broadens the distribution of local active motifs, statistically increasing the number of near-optimal Ni-centered sites for urea adsorption/activation.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: The CN-bridged M–N≡C–Fe framework is mechanically and chemically robust in alkaline electrolytes. High-entropy mixing increases the configurational entropy and suppresses cation migration and phase segregation, mitigating dissolution under potential cycling. Zn2+ is particularly effective at suppressing cation leaching and stabilizing the PBA lattice; Mn contributes by distributing strain from redox cycling across multiple sites. A controlled density of [Fe(CN)6] vacancies and interstitial water provides buffering of volume changes and enhances mass transport.
- Cost and environmental profile: All five metals (Ni, Fe, Co, Mn, Zn) are earth-abundant and relatively inexpensive; Fe and Mn are especially low-cost and environmentally benign. Co is kept at a modest level to balance performance with health/supply concerns. We avoid Ru, Ir, Pt (cost/scarcity), and Cr (hexavalent Cr risks), and we also avoid a high Cu fraction due to potential ammine-complex-driven dissolution in alkaline/urea media and stronger environmental concerns for Cu–cyanide species.
- Synthetic feasibility: One-pot aqueous co-precipitation at room temperature using K3[Fe(CN)6]/K4[Fe(CN)6] and common metal salts (nitrates/sulfates/chlorides), with mild chelation (e.g., citrate) to slow nucleation, yields uniform HE-PBA nanocubes or nanoparticles. The method is scalable, binder-friendly, and compatible with in situ growth on Ni foam or carbon cloth for low-ohmic, mechanically robust electrodes.

Element-by-element rationale

1) Ni (primary active center for UOR)
- Catalytic role: Ni2+ in the PBA lattice oxidizes to γ-NiOOH-like states under anodic polarization in alkaline media. These high-valent Ni sites drive the UOR via deprotonation and activation of the urea molecule at the NiOOH/OH− interface. Ni-based oxyhydroxides are the state of the art for UOR.
- Stability: In a CN-bridged framework, Ni sites are structurally constrained, limiting agglomeration or uncontrolled growth of NiOOH, which reduces pulverization during cycling. The HE environment disperses Ni, mitigating localized over-oxidation.
- Cost/environment: Abundant and moderate cost; manageable toxicity profile relative to precious metals.
- Synthesis: Ni2+ salts readily form PBAs with [Fe(CN)6]3−/4− under mild, aqueous conditions; kinetic control via temperature/chelators allows uniform incorporation.
- Structural features: Ni prefers octahedral N-coordination in PBAs; its presence tunes M–N≡C–Fe bond angles and the eg occupancy relevant to redox activity.

2) Fe (cyanometallate node and electronic promoter)
- Catalytic role: Although Fe on the cyanometallate sublattice is not the primary redox center for UOR, Fe strongly couples electronically to N-coordinated metals via π-backbonding of CN. Fe in the framework shifts the Ni2+/Ni3+ redox potential to more favorable values and increases covalency, often enhancing UOR/OER kinetics in Ni-based catalysts.
- Stability: [Fe(CN)6]3−/4− is the most widely used and stable cyanometallate for PBAs, lending structural robustness and tolerance to alkaline media. Fe is less prone to dissolution than many alternatives and helps preserve long-range order.
- Cost/environment: Very low cost and highly benign; avoids the environmental liabilities of Cr cyanides or rare-metal cyanides.
- Synthesis: Readily available K3[Fe(CN)6]/K4[Fe(CN)6] enables consistent, reproducible crystallization pathways at scale.
- Structural features: Fe(CN)6 octahedra form strong, linear CN bridges, enhancing mechanical integrity, while tolerating [Fe(CN)6] vacancies that introduce porosity and mass-transport pathways.

3) Co (conductivity and redox synergist, used sparingly)
- Catalytic role: Co improves electrical conductivity and facilitates multi-electron transfer processes. In Ni–Co systems, Co can stabilize high-valent Ni states and provide complementary adsorption sites, lowering UOR onset and Tafel slopes. Co also helps tune the M–N bond strength, affecting urea and OH− adsorption energetics.
- Stability: Dispersed at high entropy, Co is less prone to dissolution than in simple binary alloys/oxides. The PBA framework and presence of Zn/Mn buffer redox strain around Co sites.
- Cost/environment: More abundant and cheaper than noble metals but with higher health and supply-chain concerns than Fe/Mn/Zn; therefore, maintain moderate loading (e.g., ~20% of the N-coordinated sublattice).
- Synthesis: Co2+ incorporates smoothly in PBA lattices alongside Ni2+, allowing homogeneous HE mixing.
- Structural features: Co varies between 2+/3+ and can modulate local ligand field, fine-tuning the Ni electronic structure and broadening the distribution of active local motifs.

4) Mn (redox flexibility, lattice distortion management, oxygen-ligand tuning)
- Catalytic role: Mn’s accessible valence states (II/III/IV) adjust the redox landscape near Ni, facilitating the formation of active NiOOH and potentially assisting in lattice-oxygen participation when beneficial. Mn can weaken overly strong adsorption on Ni sites, balancing activity.
- Stability: Mn’s presence distributes lattice strain caused by repeated redox cycling, reducing microcracking and dissolution. It also helps pin defects around [Fe(CN)6] vacancies.
- Cost/environment: Very low cost and environmentally benign, improving the overall sustainability profile.
- Synthesis: Mn2+ is readily co-precipitated into PBAs. While hexacyanomanganate is less robust than hexacyanoferrate, using Fe(CN)6 as the node circumvents that issue.
- Structural features: Mn slightly distorts local octahedra, increasing configurational disorder that, in a high-entropy context, enhances stability (entropy-driven phase stabilization) and provides a richer landscape of active sites.

5) Zn (environmentally benign, stable-valence structural stabilizer)
- Catalytic role: Zn2+ is largely redox-inactive under UOR conditions, but it plays a critical indirect role by diluting highly redox-active neighbors, creating a wider ensemble of Ni-centric environments with tuned ligand fields. It can also promote the formation of beneficial vacancies and porosity without compromising lattice integrity.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: Zn2+ strongly resists oxidative dissolution in the PBA lattice under alkaline potentials and suppresses cation migration. Its stable valence state and benign chemistry reduce risks of harmful dissolution products. Zn effectively counters Jahn–Teller-type distortions that can be triggered by certain active metals, improving cycling stability.
- Cost/environment: Very abundant, low cost, and low toxicity—an ideal choice to replace a portion of more problematic metals (e.g., Cu or excess Co).
- Synthesis: Zn2+ co-precipitates cleanly with Ni/Co/Mn in PBA frameworks, aiding particle uniformity and facilitating high yields.
- Structural features: Zn introduces lattice hardening and reduces local redox strain, key for long-term alkaline durability.

Why avoid some otherwise common choices
- Cr: While CrIII can form stable hexacyanometallates, environmental and regulatory risks associated with CrVI formation argue against its inclusion.
- Ru, Ir, Pt: Excellent catalytic performance but cost, scarcity, and sustainability concerns conflict with the low-cost, environmentally friendly targets.
- Cu (optional but de-emphasized here): Although Cu can improve conductivity and tailor adsorption, in highly alkaline, amine-rich environments derived from urea, Cu can form strong ammine complexes and is more prone to leaching. Cu–cyanide complexes also carry environmental liabilities. Replacing part or all of Cu with Zn improves corrosion resistance and sustainability with only a minor penalty to activity when Ni/Fe/Co/Mn are present.

Structural and functional features that meet the brief
- High-entropy mixing on the N-coordinated sublattice (Ni/Co/Mn/Zn) maximizes configurational entropy, which:
  - Suppresses phase segregation and cation dissolution (stability, corrosion resistance)
  - Broadens the distribution of local Ni environments (activity)
  - Distributes redox/mechanical strain during cycling (durability)
- [Fe(CN)6] nodes provide a strong, linear CN bridge and can accommodate vacancies and interstitial water:
  - Vacancies increase accessible surface area and ion/water transport pathways (activity)
  - Interstitial water buffers volumetric changes (stability)
- In situ surface reconstruction under UOR potentials forms a thin, conductive, Ni/Fe/Co/Mn oxyhydroxide-rich skin on a robust PBA core:
  - Core–shell synergy combines high intrinsic activity of surface oxyhydroxides with the mechanical/ionic transport advantages of the PBA scaffold.

Synthetic route (simple, scalable, and green-leaning)
- Aqueous co-precipitation at room temperature:
  - Dissolve Ni2+, Co2+, Mn2+, and Zn2+ salts (e.g., nitrates/sulfates) in water with a mild chelator (citrate, EDTA-lite) to slow nucleation.
  - Add K3[Fe(CN)6] (and/or K4[Fe(CN)6]) dropwise under vigorous stirring at pH ~6–8; control temperature (20–30°C) for uniform nucleation.
  - Age, collect, and wash thoroughly; dry at ≤80°C. Optional low-temperature anneal (120–150°C) to adjust water content and crystallinity.
  - For electrodes, grow in situ on Ni foam or carbon cloth to reduce contact resistance; mild electrochemical activation will generate the active oxyhydroxide surface.
- Safety: Keep synthesis and operation strictly alkaline/neutral to avoid HCN release; Fe(CN)6-based PBAs are highly insoluble and safe when handled at proper pH.

Expected performance profile
- Activity: Low UOR onset potential and favorable Tafel slope due to Ni-OOH dominance, Fe/Co promotion, and Mn tuning; adequate conductivity through Co inclusion and conductive substrate; high site density from porous, vacancy-rich PBA.
- Durability: HE stabilization, Zn’s corrosion resistance, and robust CN-linked framework yield stable currents over long chronopotentiometry/chronovoltammetry runs with minimal performance decay.
- Cost-effectiveness and environmental friendliness: All components are abundant; the design minimizes reliance on problematic or expensive elements; synthesis is water-based and low-temperature.

Summary
A five-metal HE-PBA comprising Ni, Fe, Co, Mn, and Zn balances activity, stability, cost, and environmental responsibility for alkaline urea oxidation. Ni provides the main active redox center; Fe (as [Fe(CN)6]) supplies a robust, low-cost scaffold and electronic promotion; Co enhances conductivity and redox synergy (kept modest for sustainability); Mn adds redox flexibility and strain management; and the deliberate, environmentally benign inclusion of Zn2+ stabilizes the lattice and improves corrosion resistance. This composition leverages high-entropy effects and the well-established synthetic tractability of Fe-based PBAs to deliver a practical, scalable catalyst for UOR.
