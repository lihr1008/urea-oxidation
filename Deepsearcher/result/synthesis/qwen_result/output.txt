**Synthesis Protocol for a High-Entropy Prussian Blue Analog (HE-PBA) Catalyst Using K₃[Co(CN)₆] as the Cyanide Source and Incorporating Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, and Zn**

This detailed co-precipitation protocol is designed to synthesize a phase-pure, catalytically active high-entropy Prussian blue analog (HE-PBA) with equimolar incorporation of five transition metals (Fe²⁺, Co²⁺, Ni²⁺, Mn²⁺, Zn²⁺) into the nitrogen-coordinated M sites of a framework derived from potassium hexacyanocobaltate(III), K₃[Co(CN)₆]. The method leverages chelation control, inert atmosphere handling, and precise stoichiometry to ensure homogeneous metal distribution, minimal vacancies, and high crystallinity.

---

### **1. Conceptual Background**
- **HE-PBA Definition**: A single-phase Prussian blue analog where ≥5 cations randomly occupy the same crystallographic site (typically the M²⁺/³⁺ site coordinated to N of –CN–), generating configurational entropy >1.5R. This stabilizes the lattice and enhances catalytic/electrochemical properties via the “cocktail effect.”
- **Framework Source**: K₃[Co(CN)₆] provides [Coᴵᴵᴵ(CN)₆]³⁻ units, where Coᴵᴵᴵ remains in the carbon-coordinated site, while the five target metals (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Zn) occupy the nitrogen-coordinated interstitial sites.
- **Key Challenge**: Differing precipitation kinetics and complex stabilities of the five metal ions require chelation (e.g., sodium citrate) to synchronize their release and ensure atomic-level mixing.

---

### **2. Reagents and Preparation Conditions**
**All solutions must be prepared under inert atmosphere (Ar or N₂) to prevent oxidation of Fe²⁺/Mn²⁺/Co²⁺. Use deoxygenated ultrapure water.**

#### **Solution A (Cyanometal Precursor)**  
- Dissolve **15 mmol K₃[Co(CN)₆]** in **100 mL deionized (DI) water**.  
- Stir for 30 min under Ar bubbling.

#### **Solutions B₁–B₅ (Metal Salt + Chelator Solutions)**  
Prepare equimolar metal:citrate solutions (1:1 molar ratio) to form stable [M–citrate] complexes that modulate ion release:

| Solution | Metal Salt (mmol) | Hydrate Form | Sodium Citrate (mmol) | Volume (mL DI water) |
|----------|-------------------|--------------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| B₁       | NiCl₂             | 6H₂O         | 3.0                    | 75                   |
| B₂       | CoCl₂             | 6H₂O         | 3.0                    | 75                   |
| B₃       | FeCl₂             | 6H₂O         | 3.0                    | 75                   |
| B₄       | MnCl₂             | 4H₂O         | 3.0                    | 75                   |
| B₅       | ZnCl₂             | —            | 3.0                    | 75                   |

> **Note**: Total metal = 15 mmol (3 mmol each × 5 metals). Citrate ensures uniform nucleation by slowing metal ion availability per Irving–Williams stability trends ([Ni–citrate] > [Co–citrate] > [Fe–citrate] > [Mn–citrate] > [Zn–citrate]).

- Bubble each solution with Ar for **30 min**, then stir for **3 h** to fully form metal–citrate chelates.
- Combine B₁–B₅ into a single **Solution B** (total volume ≈ 375 mL).

---

### **3. Co-Precipitation Procedure**
1. Place **100 mL DI water** in a three-neck flask equipped with magnetic stirrer, gas inlet, and two addition funnels.
2. Purge with **Ar for 30 min** to maintain oxygen-free conditions.
3. Simultaneously and **slowly dropwise add**:
   - **Solution A** (K₃[Co(CN)₆]) via one funnel,
   - **Solution B** (mixed metal–citrate) via the other funnel,
   over **60–90 minutes** at **room temperature (~25°C)**.
4. Maintain **vigorous stirring** and continuous **Ar flow** throughout addition.
5. After addition, continue stirring for **2 hours** to complete nucleation.
6. **Age the suspension** undisturbed at **room temperature for 24 hours** under Ar.

> **Critical Parameters**:
> - **pH**: Naturally ~5–6; avoid external pH adjustment to prevent hydrolysis.
> - **Temperature**: Room temp prevents rapid precipitation and vacancy formation.
> - **Mixing Rate**: Slow addition ensures supersaturation is controlled, favoring single-phase HE-PBA over segregated phases.

---

### **4. Post-Synthesis Processing**
1. **Collect precipitate** by centrifugation (8,000 rpm, 10 min).
2. **Wash 3×** with DI water (deoxygenated) and 2× with anhydrous ethanol to remove residual salts and citrate.
3. **Dry** under **vacuum at 70°C overnight**.
4. **Activate** by further drying at **100°C under vacuum for 24 h** to remove lattice water while preserving framework integrity.
5. **Grind** gently to fine powder for catalytic use.

---

### **5. Characterization for Validation**
Confirm successful HE-PBA formation using:

- **PXRD**: Match to cubic PBA structure (space group *Fm*\(\bar{3}\)*m*); absence of secondary phases.
- **ICP-OES/MS**: Verify equimolar (±5%) ratios of Fe:Co:Ni:Mn:Zn in final product.
- **SEM/TEM + EDS Mapping**: Homogeneous elemental distribution across particles.
- **XAFS (XANES/EXAFS)**: Confirm all five metals are in +2 state and coordinated to N (not O/OH).
- **FTIR**: Peaks at ~2,090 cm⁻¹ (C≡N stretch) confirm intact cyanide bridges.
- **BET Surface Area**: Typically 50–150 m²/g; indicates porosity suitable for catalysis.
- **TGA**: Quantify hydration level (target: nH₂O ≈ 14–18 per formula unit).

---

### **6. Key Advantages of Using K₃[Co(CN)₆] vs. Na₄[Fe(CN)₆]**
- **Lower Vacancy Concentration**: [Coᴵᴵᴵ(CN)₆]³⁻ is more kinetically inert than [Feᴵᴵ(CN)₆]⁴⁻, reducing [Fe(CN)₆] vacancy formation.
- **Stable Oxidation State**: Coᴵᴵᴵ does not participate in redox during synthesis, simplifying control over M-site occupancy.
- **Enhanced Crystallinity**: Slower reaction kinetics promote larger, more ordered crystals.

---

### **7. Catalytic Readiness**
The resulting HE-PBA is ready for use as a catalyst (e.g., in oxygen evolution, pollutant degradation, or battery redox mediation). For enhanced activity, optional post-treatments include:
- **Controlled thermal annealing** (<300°C in inert gas) to tune defect density.
- **Acid washing** (mild HNO₃) to remove surface carbonates without collapsing the framework.

This protocol yields a **single-phase, high-entropy, vacancy-minimized HE-PBA** with maximized configurational entropy and catalytic multifunctionality derived from five synergistic transition metals.
