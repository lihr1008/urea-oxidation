### **Synthesis Protocol: (FeCoNiMnZn)-Hexacyanocobaltate HE-PBA**

**Target Material:** High-Entropy Prussian Blue Analog
**General Formula:** $(Fe_{0.2}Co_{0.2}Ni_{0.2}Mn_{0.2}Zn_{0.2})_3[Co(CN)_6]_2 \cdot nH_2O$
**Method:** Chelating-Agent Assisted Co-Precipitation

---

### **1. Reagents and Materials**

*   **Precursor (Framework Source):** Potassium Hexacyanocobaltate(III) ($K_3[Co(CN)_6]$)
*   **Metal Sources (High-Entropy Cations):**
    *   Iron(II) Chloride ($FeCl_2 \cdot 4H_2O$) or Nitrate
    *   Cobalt(II) Chloride ($CoCl_2 \cdot 6H_2O$) or Nitrate
    *   Nickel(II) Chloride ($NiCl_2 \cdot 6H_2O$) or Nitrate
    *   Manganese(II) Chloride ($MnCl_2 \cdot 4H_2O$) or Nitrate
    *   Zinc(II) Chloride ($ZnCl_2$) or Nitrate
*   **Chelating Agent:** Sodium Citrate ($Na_3C_6H_5O_7 \cdot 2H_2O$)
    *   *Note:* Sodium citrate is crucial for high-entropy synthesis. It slows down the reaction rate (slow nucleation) to ensure all five metals precipitate simultaneously into a single phase rather than separating into different phases.
*   **Solvent:** Deionized (DI) Water (18.2 MΩ·cm)

---

### **2. Stoichiometric Calculations (The Equimolar Principle)**

To achieve high configuration entropy ($\Delta S_{conf} > 1.5R$), the five transition metals must be present in nearly equal molar ratios.

*   **Total Metal Concentration:** You must determine the total moles of transition metals ("M") required to react with the hexacyanocobaltate.
*   **Molar Fraction:** Each metal (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Zn) will constitute 20% (0.2 mole fraction) of the total cationic solution.
*   **Example Calculation (for approx. 10 mmol Total Metal):**
    *   Fe precursor: 2.0 mmol
    *   Co precursor: 2.0 mmol
    *   Ni precursor: 2.0 mmol
    *   Mn precursor: 2.0 mmol
    *   Zn precursor: 2.0 mmol
    *   **Total Cations (Solution A):** 10.0 mmol

---

### **3. Step-by-Step Procedure**

#### **Step A: Preparation of the Mixed Metal Solution (Cation Solution)**
1.  **Dissolution:** In a beaker containing 100 mL of DI water, dissolve 2.0 mmol of **each** metal salt (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Zn).
2.  **Chelation:** Add Sodium Citrate to this solution. The molar ratio of Citrate to Total Metal Ions should be typically 1:1 or slightly higher (e.g., 10-15 mmol of citrate for 10 mmol of total metal) to ensure full complexation.
3.  **Mixing:** Stir vigorously until a clear, homogeneous solution is formed.
    *   *Critical Step:* If using Mn or Fe(II), it is highly recommended to bubble the solution with Argon (Ar) or Nitrogen ($N_2$) gas for 30 minutes to prevent premature oxidation of the metal ions before the crystal lattice forms.

#### **Step B: Preparation of the Framework Solution (Anion Solution)**
1.  Dissolve Potassium Hexacyanocobaltate ($K_3[Co(CN)_6]$) in 100 mL of DI water.
2.  **Stoichiometry:** The amount should correspond to the charge balance of the target PBA. For divalent metals ($M^{2+}$) and trivalent hexacyanocobaltate ($[Co(CN)_6]^{3-}$), the ideal ratio is $3M : 2[Co(CN)_6]$.
    *   *Calculation:* For 10 mmol total metal cations, use approx. 6.67 mmol of $K_3[Co(CN)_6]$. Often, a slight excess of the anion is used to ensure complete precipitation.

#### **Step C: Co-Precipitation Reaction**
1.  **Setup:** Place "Solution A" (Mixed Metals + Citrate) on a magnetic stirrer at room temperature ($25^\circ C$).
2.  **Addition:** Slowly add "Solution B" (Hexacyanocobaltate) into "Solution A" dropwise. A peristaltic pump or dispensing funnel is recommended to control the rate (approx. 1-2 mL/min).
    *   *Observation:* A precipitate will begin to form immediately (color depends on the dominant optical properties, often brownish/purple for this mix).
3.  **Stirring:** Maintain continuous magnetic stirring (approx. 400-600 rpm) during the addition.

#### **Step D: Aging Process**
1.  Once the addition is complete, cover the beaker (e.g., with Parafilm or a lid).
2.  Allow the suspension to **age** at room temperature for **24 hours**.
    *   *Purpose:* Aging improves crystallinity and ensures the uniform distribution of the five elements within the lattice structure (homogenization).

#### **Step E: Washing and Collection**
1.  **Centrifugation:** Collect the precipitate by centrifugation (e.g., 8000 rpm for 10 mins). Discard the supernatant.
2.  **Washing:** Re-disperse the solid in DI water and centrifuge again. Repeat this water wash 2-3 times to remove excess Potassium ($K^+$), Sodium ($Na^+$) from the citrate, and unreacted ions.
3.  **Solvent Exchange:** Perform a final wash with Ethanol. This helps remove water from the pores and prevents agglomeration during drying.

#### **Step F: Drying and Post-Treatment**
1.  **Drying:** Dry the obtained powder in a vacuum oven at **60°C to 80°C** for 12–24 hours. High temperatures (>150°C) should be avoided to prevent the loss of coordinated water which stabilizes the framework, unless a dehydrated phase is specifically desired.
2.  **Grinding:** Grind the dried product into a fine powder using a mortar and pestle.

### **4. Key Synthesis Considerations for this Specific HE-PBA**

*   **Role of Zinc (Zn):** Zinc reacts faster than other metals in PBAs. The use of Sodium Citrate is particularly important here to retard the reaction of Zn, preventing it from precipitating alone and ensuring it integrates into the high-entropy lattice.
*   **Oxidation Prevention:** Since Iron (Fe) and Manganese (Mn) are prone to oxidation (from +2 to +3) in air, performing the mixing and aging under an inert atmosphere (Argon bubbling) is recommended if strictly controlling the valence state is required for catalytic performance.
*   **Yield:** This protocol typically yields a powder that can be characterized by XRD (to confirm a single Face-Centered Cubic phase) and EDS mapping (to confirm the uniform distribution of all 5 metals).
