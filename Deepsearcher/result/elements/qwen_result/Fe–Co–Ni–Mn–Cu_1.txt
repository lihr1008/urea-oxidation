**Design of a High-Entropy Prussian Blue Analog Catalyst for Alkaline Urea Oxidation: Rationale for Five-Metal Selection**

To meet the stringent requirements of high catalytic activity, structural stability, low cost, ease of synthesis, environmental benignity, and corrosion resistance in alkaline media, a five-metal high-entropy Prussian blue analog (HE-PBA) is proposed with the composition: **Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, and Cu**. This selection is grounded in recent advances in multi-metal PBAs for energy storage and emerging evidence of their applicability to electrocatalysis, particularly urea oxidation.

### 1. **Iron (Fe)**  
- **Catalytic Role**: Fe centers in PBAs are redox-active in alkaline media and participate in facilitating charge transfer during UOR. Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ couples contribute to electron conduction and surface reactivity.
- **Stability & Structure**: Fe is integral to the PBA framework as it commonly occupies the [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻/³⁻ sites, providing the cyanide-bridged backbone essential for structural integrity.
- **Cost & Synthesis**: Iron salts (e.g., K₃[Fe(CN)₆]) are inexpensive, abundant, and compatible with aqueous co-precipitation—a standard, scalable PBA synthesis method.
- **Environmental Impact**: Non-toxic and naturally abundant; poses minimal ecological risk.

### 2. **Cobalt (Co)**  
- **Catalytic Role**: Co is highly active for UOR due to its ability to form CoOOH species under anodic conditions, which serve as the true catalytic phase for urea dehydrogenation and N–H bond cleavage.
- **Redox Flexibility**: Co²⁺/Co³⁺ transitions are facile in alkaline electrolytes, enhancing charge transfer kinetics.
- **Structural Contribution**: Co incorporation strengthens the metal–N coordination network and improves electronic conductivity.
- **Trade-offs**: Moderately priced but justified by performance gains; globally available and widely used in electrocatalysts.

### 3. **Nickel (Ni)**  
- **Catalytic Role**: Ni is the benchmark UOR catalyst (e.g., in Ni(OH)₂/NiOOH systems). In HE-PBAs, Ni²⁺ oxidizes to Ni³⁺ under operating conditions, forming active NiOOH sites that adsorb and oxidize urea efficiently.
- **Synergistic Effects**: Works cooperatively with Co and Fe to lower the onset potential and increase current density.
- **Stability**: Ni enhances lattice rigidity and mitigates Jahn–Teller distortions from other metals (e.g., Mn³⁺).
- **Cost & Safety**: Low-cost, earth-abundant, and environmentally acceptable.

### 4. **Manganese (Mn)**  
- **Catalytic Role**: Mn contributes through Mn²⁺/Mn³⁺/Mn⁴⁺ redox chemistry, promoting oxygen evolution side-reactions that can assist in maintaining surface cleanliness during UOR.
- **Structural Stabilization**: Despite potential Jahn–Teller distortion (from Mn³⁺), in a high-entropy environment, lattice strain is distributed across multiple cations, suppressing phase segregation and enhancing entropy-driven stability.
- **Environmental Profile**: Mn is non-toxic, abundant, and commonly used in green battery chemistries.
- **Cost Advantage**: One of the cheapest transition metals, ideal for large-scale deployment.

### 5. **Copper (Cu)**  
- **Rationale for Inclusion**: Cu is the “tentative environmentally benign metal with stable valence states” referenced in the query. While Cu⁺ is unstable in water, Cu²⁺ is highly stable in alkaline PBA frameworks.
- **Electronic Modulation**: Cu²⁺ introduces localized electronic states that improve overall conductivity and modify d-band centers of neighboring metals, optimizing urea adsorption energy.
- **Corrosion Resistance**: Cu-doped PBAs show enhanced resistance to oxidative dissolution in strong alkali, likely due to strong Cu–N bonding.
- **Cost & Sustainability**: Copper is relatively low-cost, recyclable, and less problematic than critical metals like Ru, Ir, or V.

---

### **Excluded Metals and Design Philosophy**
Metals such as **Zn**, **Cr**, **V**, or **Ru** were deliberately avoided:
- **Zn²⁺** is redox-inactive in UOR-relevant potentials and may leach in alkaline media.
- **Cr** and **V** raise toxicity and environmental concerns.
- **Ru/Ir** offer high activity but are prohibitively expensive and scarce.

The inclusion of **Cu**—as a stable, benign, and electronically beneficial element—optimizes the entropy-stabilized structure without compromising sustainability.

---

### **High-Entropy Advantages Confirmed by Literature**
Recent studies (e.g., Qian et al., *Chem. Eng. J.* 2024; Chou & Tuan, *Adv. Funct. Mater.* 2025) demonstrate that:
- Increasing metal diversity (up to 5–6 elements) in PBAs enhances configurational entropy, leading to “quasi-zero-strain” frameworks during ion insertion/extraction.
- Multi-metal synergy (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Cu) distributes redox activity, suppresses phase transitions, and improves long-term cycling—principles directly transferable to UOR stability.
- DFT calculations confirm that high-entropy PBAs exhibit reduced diffusion barriers and improved electronic conductivity—key for fast UOR kinetics.

---

### **Conclusion**
The proposed **Fe–Co–Ni–Mn–Cu** high-entropy PBA satisfies all design criteria:
- **High UOR activity** via synergistic redox couples (Ni/Co dominant, supported by Fe/Mn/Cu).
- **Exceptional structural stability** from entropy stabilization and balanced ionic radii.
- **Low cost and scalability** using aqueous co-precipitation with commodity metal salts.
- **Environmental compatibility** with no toxic or critical raw materials.
- **Corrosion resistance** in concentrated KOH due to robust M–N≡C–Fe linkages and stable Cu²⁺ integration.

This composition represents a rational, literature-informed, and application-targeted catalyst design for efficient and sustainable urea-based energy conversion systems.
