### **Proposed Catalyst Composition: Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, and Zn**

This composition balances high catalytic activity, structural stability, cost-effectiveness, ease of synthesis, environmental friendliness, and corrosion resistance. The introduction of **Zinc (Zn)** serves as the environmentally benign metal with a stable valence state (Zn²⁺) to optimize performance.

---

### **Detailed Rationale for Metal Selection**

#### **1. Iron (Fe)**
*   **Catalytic Activity & Structural Role:** As the core constituent of the Prussian blue framework (coordinating with cyanide bridges), Fe ensures structural integrity. Fe centers, particularly in high oxidation states (Fe³⁺/Fe⁴⁺), can participate in redox cycles, contributing to electrocatalytic activity.
*   **Stability & Synthetic Feasibility:** Fe-based PBAs are known for their robust framework stability in aqueous and alkaline media. Iron salts are extremely cheap and readily form PBAs via simple co-precipitation.
*   **Cost & Environmental Impact:** Iron is the most abundant and inexpensive transition metal, making it highly cost-effective and environmentally benign.

#### **2. Cobalt (Co)**
*   **Catalytic Activity:** Cobalt is a premier non-noble metal for oxidation reactions. In UOR, Co sites are highly active for the adsorption and oxidation of urea molecules. Co³⁺/Co⁴⁺ redox couples are crucial for the electrochemical oxidation process.
*   **Synergistic Effect:** Co synergizes with Ni and Fe, often leading to enhanced electron transfer and optimized adsorption energies for reaction intermediates.
*   **Cost Consideration:** While more expensive than Fe or Ni, Co is essential for high activity. Its inclusion in a multi-metal, high-entropy formulation reduces the overall catalyst cost per mass compared to a Co-rich catalyst.

#### **3. Nickel (Ni)**
*   **Catalytic Activity:** Ni is another outstanding UOR catalyst in alkaline media. The Ni²⁺/Ni³⁺ redox transition is fundamental to urea electrooxidation. Its presence creates highly active sites.
*   **Synergy & Conductivity:** Ni exhibits strong synergistic effects with Co, often forming Co-Ni dual-active sites that are more effective than single metals. It also helps improve the overall electrical conductivity of the catalyst.
*   **Cost & Stability:** Nickel is relatively low-cost and contributes to the long-term structural and catalytic stability of the PBA framework.

#### **4. Copper (Cu)**
*   **Electronic Structure Modulation:** The addition of Cu tunes the electronic structure of the HE-PBA. As shown in related research, Cu-doping effectively boosts the electrical conductivity of the material, facilitating faster charge transfer during electrocatalysis.
*   **Structural Modulation:** Cu influences particle size and morphology during synthesis. Its distinct ionic radius and coordination preference introduce beneficial lattice strain and defects in the high-entropy structure, which can expose more active sites.
*   **Cost & Environmental Profile:** Copper is moderately priced and widely available. While its extraction has environmental impacts, its use in a catalyst is minimal and not a primary toxicity concern.

#### **5. Zinc (Zn) - The Introduced Optimizing Element**
*   **Rationale for Introduction:** Zn is introduced to replace a less environmentally ideal or more costly metal (e.g., Mn or a higher Co content). It fulfills the criteria of having a stable valence state (Zn²⁺, not redox-active under UOR conditions) and being highly environmentally benign.
*   **Role in Stability and Structure:**
    *   **Defect Modulation:** As evidenced in the literature, Zn-doping is particularly effective in modulating the formation of hexacyanoferrate vacancies in the PBA lattice. Controlled vacancies can enhance ion diffusion and increase the number of accessible active sites.
    *   **Structural Stabilizer:** The stable Zn²⁺ ion can occupy framework sites, helping to maintain the structural integrity of the PBA during prolonged cycling in corrosive alkaline electrolytes, thereby enhancing corrosion resistance.
    *   **Synergistic Effect with Cu:** Co-doping with Cu and Zn can synergistically tune particle size, specific surface area, and vacancy content, leading to optimized electrochemical performance.
*   **Cost & Environmental Friendliness:** Zinc is very low-cost, abundant, and has low toxicity, significantly improving the overall environmental profile and cost-effectiveness of the catalyst.

### **Overall Synergistic Advantages of the High-Entropy Design**

1.  **Enhanced Catalytic Activity:** The combination of multiple redox-active metals (Fe, Co, Ni, Cu) creates a multitude of active sites with slightly different electronic environments due to lattice distortion (the "high-entropy effect"). This can optimize the adsorption strength of urea and reaction intermediates, lowering the activation energy for UOR.
2.  **Superior Stability & Corrosion Resistance:** The high configurational entropy of the HE-PBA lattice stabilizes the structure against phase separation and degradation in alkaline media. The inclusion of stable Zn²⁺ further reinforces this. Studies on etched HE-PBAs confirm their excellent performance and stability for UOR.
3.  **Synthetic Feasibility:** All selected metals can be incorporated into a PBA framework via a simple, scalable, and cost-effective one-pot co-precipitation method, as demonstrated for multi-metal PBAs.
4.  **Meeting Cost & Environmental Goals:** The formulation prioritizes abundant metals (Fe, Ni, Zn), uses a minimal amount of more costly ones (Co), and excludes rare or highly toxic metals. The use of Zn as a structural modulator enhances performance without relying on expensive or environmentally concerning elements.

**Conclusion:** The proposed **Fe-Co-Ni-Cu-Zn high-entropy Prussian blue analog** is a rationally designed catalyst that leverages the individual strengths and synergistic interactions of five transition metals. It effectively balances the demands for high UOR activity and durability in alkaline conditions with imperative considerations of cost, synthetic ease, and environmental sustainability. The strategic inclusion of zinc is key to achieving an optimal balance between structural stability, defect engineering, and a benign environmental footprint.
