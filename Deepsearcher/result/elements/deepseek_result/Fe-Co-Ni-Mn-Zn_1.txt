A high-entropy Prussian blue analog composed of the five transition metals: **Iron (Fe), Cobalt (Co), Nickel (Ni), Manganese (Mn), and Zinc (Zn)**.

### **Rationale for Metal Selection**

This composition balances catalytic activity, structural stability, and practical requirements (cost, synthesis, environment). The introduction of **Mn** and **Zn** as elements with stable valence states and environmentally benign characteristics is a key optimization strategy.

**1. Iron (Fe) & Cobalt (Co): The High-Activity Core**
*   **Role:** Fe and Co are fundamental components of classic PBAs and are highly active for oxygen evolution reactions (OER), which share mechanistic similarities with UOR. Research shows high-entropy PBAs containing Fe and Co function as efficient trifunctional catalysts for water, ethanol, and urea electrooxidation.
*   **Contribution:** They provide strong redox activity (Fe²⁺/³⁺, Co²⁺/³⁺), which is crucial for the electrocatalytic oxidation process. Their synergy in a high-entropy structure creates a highly active catalytic matrix. They are relatively low-cost and abundant.

**2. Nickel (Ni): The UOR Activity Driver**
*   **Role:** Ni is widely recognized as the most active base metal for UOR in alkaline media. It facilitates the formation of active NiOOH species, which are directly involved in the urea oxidation mechanism.
*   **Contribution:** Incorporating Ni directly targets and enhances the catalyst's UOR-specific activity, lowering the required overpotential. Its inclusion is essential for achieving high performance for the target reaction.

**3. Manganese (Mn): The Structural Stabilizer and Environmental Optimizer**
*   **Role:** Mn is introduced as the metal with stable valence states (e.g., Mn²⁺, Mn³⁺/⁴⁺) and low environmental toxicity. Its stable electronic configuration contributes to the structural integrity of the PBA lattice.
*   **Contribution:**
    *   **Stability:** Mn helps stabilize the crystal structure during the harsh anodic UOR process, mitigating corrosion and degradation. The "high entropy stabilization" effect, as noted in related high-entropy materials, is enhanced by incorporating Mn.
    *   **Environmental Friendliness:** It replaces more costly or potentially toxic metals (e.g., Mo, V, Cu), aligning with the requirement for an environmentally benign catalyst.
    *   **Synergy:** It participates in the "ensemble effects," where multiple metal sites co-stabilize key reaction intermediates, as evidenced by *in situ* studies on high-entropy alloys for UOR.

**4. Zinc (Zn): The Sacrificial Template for Porosity and Corrosion Resistance**
*   **Role:** Zn²⁺ has a stable 3d¹⁰ electronic configuration and is environmentally benign. In PBA synthesis, it can act as a sacrificial template.
*   **Contribution:**
    *   **Ease of Synthesis & Activity:** During synthesis or post-treatment (e.g., NH₃·H₂O etching as shown in research), selective leaching of Zn can create intricate hollow or porous architectures. This dramatically increases the specific surface area, exposing more active sites and facilitating mass transfer, thereby boosting apparent activity.
    *   **Corrosion Resistance:** The remaining Zn or its dissolution product contributes to a stable, corrosion-resistant framework in alkaline conditions.
    *   **Cost & Environment:** Zn is extremely low-cost and non-toxic.

The porous/microstructure enabled by Zn and etching further boosts performance.
*   **Low Cost & Ease of Synthesis:** All selected metals (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Zn) are abundant and inexpensive compared to noble metals. PBAs are typically synthesized via simple co-precipitation or solvothermal methods, which are scalable and cost-effective.
