    *   **Role & Contribution:** Serves as a fundamental structural and electronic modulator. Fe ions (Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) are integral to the PBA lattice and participate in redox cycles with other metals (e.g., Mn), enhancing electron transfer and generating active oxygen species, which is crucial for oxidation reactions.
    *   **Criteria Met:** **Low cost** and highly abundant. **Environmentally benign**. Contributes to **structural stability** within the PBA framework. Its synthesis into PBAs is well-established and straightforward.

2.  **Cobalt (Co)**
    *   **Role & Contribution:** A highly active center for oxidation reactions. Co sites, particularly in high-valence states (Co³⁺/Co⁴⁺), are excellent for O-H bond cleavage and are pivotal for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER), which often couples with UOR kinetics. Its inclusion boosts the overall **catalytic activity**.
    *   **Criteria Met:** Although more expensive than Fe or Ni, its high performance justifies its inclusion in a multi-metal system where a lower overall loading can be used. It is **synthetically feasible** in PBA structures.

3.  **Nickel (Ni)**
    *   **Role & Contribution:** The **primary active site for urea oxidation**. Ni²⁺/Ni³⁺ is the most established redox couple for catalyzing UOR in alkaline media. Its presence is non-negotiable for achieving high UOR activity and current density, as confirmed by studies on nickel-based oxides and PBAs.
    *   **Criteria Met:** **Low cost**, abundant, and exhibits good **corrosion resistance** in alkaline electrolytes. The synthesis of Ni-containing PBAs is simple and scalable.

4.  **Copper (Cu)**
    *   **Role & Contribution:** Functions as an **electronic structure and conductivity modulator**. Doping with Cu is known to significantly enhance the electrical conductivity of the PBA lattice, facilitating faster electron transfer during electrocatalysis. It can also help tune particle size and specific surface area.
    *   **Criteria Met:** **Low cost** and abundant. Its addition improves **synthetic control** over morphology. It is generally considered to have lower environmental toxicity compared to some other heavy metals.

5.  **Manganese (Mn) - The Introduced Optimizing Element**
    *   **Role & Contribution:** This element is introduced as the **stabilizing and environmentally benign optimizer**. Mn possesses multiple **stable valence states** (Mn²⁺, Mn³⁺, Mn⁴⁺), enabling rich redox chemistry that can synergize with Fe and Ni cycles, potentially lowering the energy barrier for intermediate steps. It contributes to **structural stability** and can modulate the formation of vacancies in the PBA lattice. As a non-precious, low-toxicity metal, it aligns perfectly with environmental and cost goals.
    *   **Criteria Met:** **Low cost**, **abundant**, and **environmentally friendly**. Its inclusion enhances the **high-entropy effect**, which is linked to improved catalytic stability and corrosion resistance by suppressing phase segregation and mitigating dissolution.
