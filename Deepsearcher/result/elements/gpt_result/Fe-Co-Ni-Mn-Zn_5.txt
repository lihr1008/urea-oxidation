Summary report: Five-metal high-entropy Prussian blue analog catalyst for alkaline urea oxidation (UOR), with detailed element selection rationale, structure–property relationships, synthesis approach, and stability/safety considerations

Target composition and crystal chemistry
- Framework: mixed-divalent A-site metals [(Ni, Co, Mn, Zn)] coordinated to N of cyanide; B-site hexacyanoferrate [Fe(CN)6] coordinated to C. Representative formula: (Ni,Co,Mn,Zn)3[Fe(CN)6]2·nH2O with controlled [Fe(CN)6] vacancies and crystal water.
- Role of high-entropy mixing: Increasing configurational entropy across four A-site metals reduces lattice distortions and strain during redox cycling, improving mechanical integrity and cycle life. Evidence from high-entropy PBAs in batteries shows performance increases with entropy and quasi-zero-strain behavior during cycling; Ni and Zn act as structure stabilizers, while Mn, Fe, and Co contribute electrochemical activity.
- Defect/hydration engineering: Minimizing [Fe(CN)6] vacancies enhances load-bearing capacity, ion transport, and long-term stability; appropriate crystal water content maintains framework flexibility and buffers volume changes. Studies on Cu–Fe PBAs confirm fewer vacancies improve rate capability and ultralong cycle life.

Element-by-element rationale
1) Nickel (Ni)
- Catalytic activity: Ni is the primary UOR-active center in alkaline media. In Ni–Fe PBAs, in situ Raman shows Ni can maintain a stable divalent state (Ni(II)) for extended periods during initial UOR, indicating a unique active intermediate and robust catalytic behavior. The Ni–Fe bimetallic center substantially enhances urea electro-oxidation.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: Under alkaline polarization, surface Ni(OH)2/NiOOH layers form that both participate in catalysis and passivate against corrosion, preserving the cyanide-linked framework beneath.
- Synthetic feasibility and cost: Abundant, moderate cost; readily incorporated via room-temperature aqueous co-precipitation; widely used in PBA syntheses.
- Structural features: Ni(II) in octahedral N-coordination with cyanide provides strong-field interactions that tune electronic structure for favorable adsorption and reaction kinetics.

2) Iron (Fe) at the B-site (hexacyanoferrate)
- Catalytic/electronic contribution: Fe(CN)6 units provide strong-field cyanide ligands that stabilize the lattice and modulate the A-site metal electronic structure, enabling synergistic charge transfer with Ni and Co. Mixed FeIII/FeII states can participate in electron buffering during UOR transients.
- Stability and cost-effectiveness: Iron is abundant and low-cost; the hexacyanoferrate framework is well-established and robust when cyanide remains ligand-bound.
- Environmental profile: Fe–cyanide coordination is strong; with proper synthesis and operation, free cyanide is minimized. Fe at the B-site is standard for environmentally benign PBAs relative to noble-metal alternatives.
- Structural features: The Fe(CN)6 units define the open framework porosity and ion-accessible channels; careful control of vacancy content and hydration around Fe centers is key to durability.

3) Cobalt (Co)
- Catalytic activity: Co provides fast redox kinetics (Co2+/Co3+) and improves conductivity, accelerating UOR electron transfer. In mixed Ni–Co systems, Co often lowers activation barriers and increases site turnover.
- Stability/corrosion resistance: CoOOH-like surface phases generated in alkaline potentials contribute both catalytic function and passivation, protecting the PBA interior under UOR bias.
- Synthetic feasibility/cost: Abundant, moderate cost; readily co-precipitated with Ni, Mn, Zn; commonly used in PBA and oxide electrocatalysts.
- Structural features: Co in N-coordination complements Ni by tuning local ligand-field strength and d-electron occupancy, broadening active-site diversity in a high-entropy matrix.

4) Manganese (Mn)
- Catalytic activity: Mn offers multivalence (Mn2+/Mn3+/Mn4+) and high-spin states that modulate adsorption energies of urea-derived intermediates and oxygen-containing species, often enhancing kinetics when synergizing with Ni/Co/Fe.
- Stability: While Mn3+ can introduce Jahn–Teller distortions, high-entropy mixing disperses and suppresses such local distortions, contributing to quasi-zero-strain behavior and improved cycle stability seen in multi-metal PBAs.
- Synthetic feasibility/cost/environment: Very abundant and low-cost; environmentally benign; co-precipitates well in aqueous systems.
- Structural features: Mn broadens the distribution of local electronic environments, increasing the heterogeneity of active sites and enabling defect and microstrain tuning beneficial for UOR.

5) Zinc (Zn)
- Structural stabilization: Zn2+ is largely redox-inert under UOR conditions but acts as a lattice stabilizer that reduces [Fe(CN)6] vacancy formation, strengthens metal–ligand bonding networks, and suppresses dissolution in alkaline media. High-entropy studies indicate Zn contributes prominently to structural stability.
- Corrosion resistance: Zn incorporation can increase the mechanical robustness of the framework and lower susceptibility to alkaline corrosion. Careful, sub-stoichiometric Zn fractions prevent detrimental interfacial redox phenomena reported for Zn-only PBAs in certain battery electrolytes.
- Synthetic feasibility/cost/environment: Very low cost, earth-abundant, and environmentally benign; easily co-precipitated; helps meet sustainability constraints by avoiding noble/toxic metals.
- Structural features: Zn’s closed-shell, redox-silent character in N-coordination stabilizes local lattice sites while maintaining porosity and ion transport pathways optimized by the other active metals.

Why these five and what is avoided
- Metals deliberately avoided: Noble metals (Ru, Ir, Pt) and heavy/toxic species (Cr, Cd, Hg) due to cost, supply risk, and environmental concerns. Cu can be highly active in related reactions; however, to balance UOR activity with long-term alkaline stability and minimize spontaneous interfacial redox observed in some Cu- or Zn-only PBAs, the proposed set prioritizes Ni/Co/Mn/Fe for activity with Zn for stabilizing structure without introducing additional interfacial reactivity.
- Balance achieved: Ni and Fe deliver proven UOR synergy; Co and Mn amplify kinetics and broaden active-site diversity; Zn increases robustness and corrosion resistance. All are earth-abundant and compatible with scalable, aqueous syntheses.

Mechanistic and structure–property considerations for UOR
- Active-site diversity and mixed valence: High-entropy mixing produces a distribution of local ligand fields and d-electron configurations, enabling multiple catalytic microenvironments. This increases the probability of optimal binding energies for urea and intermediates while mitigating overbinding/poisoning.
- Cyanide bridging and electronic tuning: Strong Fe–C≡N–M(N) bridges create rigid, electronically coupled networks that facilitate charge transport during UOR. Fe(CN)6 ligands stabilize A-site metal valence states; notably, Ni can remain as an active Ni(II) intermediate for prolonged periods, supporting durable activity before oxyhydroxide transformation.
- Defect engineering: Controlling [Fe(CN)6] vacancies is crucial—too many vacancies degrade stability; fewer vacancies enhance conductivity and cycle life while maintaining sufficient open channels for reactant/product transport. Crystal water acts as a strain buffer and assists ion migration; a moderate hydration level is beneficial.
- Corrosion resistance in alkaline KOH: Under UOR bias, thin surface oxyhydroxide layers (NiOOH, CoOOH) form that catalyze UOR and passivate the framework against cyanide ligand degradation. Zn’s presence further lowers dissolution propensity. High-entropy mixing reduces localized strain evolution, helping maintain the framework under long-term operation.

Synthesis and processing routes
- Scalable, green co-precipitation: Prepare an aqueous solution of mixed A-site metal salts (e.g., chlorides or nitrates of Ni2+, Co2+, Mn2+, Zn2+) with a mild chelating agent (citrate) to slow nucleation and reduce vacancy formation. Under vigorous stirring at room temperature, slowly add a stoichiometric or slight excess solution of K3[Fe(CN)6] to favor full B-site occupancy.
- pH and ionic strength control: Maintain near-neutral to slightly acidic pH to form uniform particles; use background salts (KCl) to manage ionic strength and promote crystallinity. Aging at room temperature allows ordering and reduces defects.
- Substrate growth and activation: In-situ growth on nickel foam via dip-coating/co-precipitation enhances adhesion, conductivity, and mechanical stability. Mild thermal aging (60–100 °C) and subsequent electrochemical activation in KOH/urea can generate protective oxyhydroxide surface layers without compromising the cyanide-linked core.
- Vacancy/hydration tuning: Adjust citrate:metal ratio and total metal:hexacyanoferrate stoichiometry to minimize [Fe(CN)6] vacancies. Control drying conditions to retain beneficial crystal water. These steps are evidenced to increase stability and rate performance in PBA electrodes.
- Optional benign coatings: Thin nitrogen-doped carbon or oxide shells deposited via low-temperature processes can further suppress dissolution while keeping mass transfer pathways open; use only if needed to meet stringent durability targets, as the base high-entropy framework should already provide enhanced stability.

Environmental safety and cyanide management
- Ligand stability: Hexacyanoferrate’s cyanide ligands are strongly bound; under typical alkaline UOR potentials and pH, cyanide release is minimal. Avoid extreme anodic potentials that can drive ligand degradation; maintain operation within the UOR window below vigorous OER.
- Synthesis safety: Use aqueous media, avoid free cyanide excess, and ensure thorough washing to remove unbound cyanide species. Effluents should be treated to convert any trace cyanide to benign complexes if present.
- Durability checks: Incorporate routine monitoring (e.g., cyanide test strips or ion chromatography of electrolytes) in long-duration tests to verify negligible leaching. High-entropy mixing and Zn stabilization reduce risk of framework breakdown that could release ligands.

Cost, sustainability, and manufacturability
- All selected metals (Ni, Co, Fe, Mn, Zn) are abundant and reasonably priced, meeting cost targets while avoiding scarce/toxic elements.
- The synthesis is water-based, low-temperature, and scalable, with minimal waste. Co-precipitation is widely used for PBAs and has been demonstrated as eco-friendly.
- The design leverages established PBA chemistry with defect and hydration control to meet durability and corrosion resistance requirements in concentrated KOH plus urea.

Expected performance profile and optimization levers
- Activity: The Ni–Fe center provides high intrinsic UOR activity in alkaline media; Co and Mn increase turnover via enhanced redox kinetics and adsorption tuning; the diverse high-entropy A-site environment broadens accessible active-site configurations.
- Stability: Reduced vacancy content, Zn-aided lattice stabilization, and formation of protective oxyhydroxide layers yield robust durability under continuous operation. High-entropy effects suppress strain and phase changes, translating to long cycle life.
- Optimization: Tune A-site ratios to balance activity and stability—e.g., Ni-rich compositions for activity (Ni 30–40%), Co/Mn as kinetic enhancers (each 15–25%), and Zn as stabilizer (10–20%). Empirically adjust vacancy/hydration via chelation and stoichiometry. Validate corrosion resistance by extended operation in 1 M KOH with 0.33 M urea, monitoring activity retention and any ligand leaching.

Overall justification
This five-metal high-entropy PBA—A-site Ni, Co, Mn, Zn with Fe at the hexacyanoferrate B-site—meets the multi-criteria design brief:
- High activity from Ni–Fe synergy and Co/Mn-driven kinetics.
- High stability from high-entropy lattice, minimized vacancies, Zn stabilization, and protective oxyhydroxide formation.
- Low cost and environmental friendliness via earth-abundant metals and green synthesis.
- Corrosion resistance in alkaline KOH derived from the robust cyanide-bridged framework and passivating surface layers.
Published evidence across PBA electrocatalysis and battery studies supports each metal’s specific contribution: Ni(II) as a durable active intermediate in UOR; Fe(CN)6 providing strong-field lattice and electronic coupling; Co/Mn increasing redox kinetics and capacity/activity; Zn and Ni stabilizing multi-metal PBAs against strain and dissolution when vacancies are controlled.
