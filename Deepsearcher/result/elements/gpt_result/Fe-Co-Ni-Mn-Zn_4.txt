Design summary and rationale for a five-metal high-entropy Prussian blue analog (PBA) catalyst for alkaline urea oxidation (UOR)

1) Performance requirements and constraints, grounded in prior evidence
- Catalytic activity: The Ni-centered redox couple (Ni2+/Ni3+ in NiOOH) is the primary driver of UOR activity in alkaline media. Co lowers overpotentials and Fe enhances activity and durability when combined with Ni, as demonstrated for multielement Ni–Co–Fe systems in urea/methanol electro-oxidation. P-dopants or oxyanions (borate/phosphate) can tune charge distribution at active sites and accelerate kinetics by facilitating interfacial proton/charge transfer during C–N bond activation in urea.
- Stability and corrosion resistance: PBA-derived Ni–Fe oxides show robust UOR performance with stable Ni3+/Ni2+ and Fe3+/Fe2+ couples, indicating Ni–Fe frameworks resist deactivation while providing abundant redox sites. Managing Ni self-oxidation (NSOR) is critical; interface/electronic tuning that makes UOR energetically favorable before NSOR improves durability.
- Cost/environmental profile: Transition metals Ni, Fe, Co, Mn, Zn are low-cost and relatively benign; noble metals (Ru, Ir, Pt, Pd) and environmentally problematic elements (e.g., Cr due to hexavalent species) should be avoided. Cu can boost activity in some systems but is prone to dissolution/complexation in alkaline media containing ammonia/urea, compromising durability.
- Ease of synthesis: Room-temperature routes (co-precipitation, sol–gel) are effective for homogeneous multimetal mixing. High-entropy systems benefit from slow, controlled nucleation to avoid phase segregation, as emphasized in multielement catalysts synthesized via solution routes.

2) Proposed high-entropy PBA composition and stoichiometry
- Framework: A-site mixed divalent metals bridged to B-site hexacyanometallate
- Composition: (Ni0.25Co0.25Mn0.25Zn0.25)3[FeIII(CN)6]2·nH2O
  - A-site (N-coordination): equimolar Ni2+, Co2+, Mn2+, Zn2+ to realize high-entropy mixing and tune the Ni-centered redox environment
  - B-site (C-coordination): FeIII(CN)6 as the robust, low-cost redox-active node
  - Charge-balanced stoichiometry MII3[FeIII(CN)6]2 with a controlled fraction of intrinsic [Fe(CN)6] vacancies and interstitial water to enhance porosity and mass transport

3) Element-by-element rationale
- Ni (primary active center)
  - Catalytic activity/redox: Ni is the canonical active site for UOR in alkaline media via Ni2+ → Ni3+ (NiOOH) transition; the NiOOH phase activates urea and facilitates C–N bond scission. Prior multimetal studies consistently place Ni at the core of activity for UOR.
  - Structural role: Occupies A-site coordinated to N in M–N≡C–Fe linkages; proximity to Fe(CN)6 units enhances charge transfer during redox.
  - Stability: In multimetal matrices, Fe and Co shift the potential for NiOOH formation and reduce susceptibility to NSOR, improving durability.
  - Cost/environment: Abundant, relatively low-cost; widely used in alkaline electrocatalysis.
  - Synthetic feasibility: Readily incorporated into PBAs by co-precipitation with K3[Fe(CN)6].

- Fe (activity booster and redox mediator)
  - Catalytic activity/redox: Fe improves activity and durability in Ni-based multimetal UOR catalysts; the Fe3+/Fe2+ couple offers an electron reservoir that stabilizes Ni redox cycling and can assist charge redistribution at the active interface.
  - Structural role: As [FeIII(CN)6]3− at the B-site, provides a rigid cyanide framework; strong Fe–C≡N bonding stabilizes the lattice and supports fast electron transfer through M–N≡C–Fe bridges. The classic Ni–N≡C–Fe linkage underpins the well-documented Ni–Fe synergy.
  - Stability: Fe–cyanide nodes produce mechanically and chemically robust networks; Fe helps buffer Ni against over-oxidation and dissolution.
  - Cost/environment: Very low cost; environmentally benign at the oxidation states used.
  - Synthetic feasibility: Standard PBA chemistry uses K3[Fe(CN)6], enabling clean, scalable synthesis at room temperature.

- Co (overpotential reducer and electronic modulator)
  - Catalytic activity/redox: Co incorporation lowers UOR overpotentials and improves conductivity; Co2+/Co3+ couples promote NiOOH formation at lower potentials and optimize adsorption energies of reaction intermediates.
  - Structural role: A-site Co2+ distorts and tunes the local field around Ni, broadening the distribution of Ni–N≡C–Fe environments that create multiple active micro-sites. This multienvironment landscape is characteristic of high-entropy PBAs.
  - Stability: By facilitating NiOOH at milder potentials, Co helps initiate UOR before deleterious NSOR pathways dominate, aligning with interface/electronic-structure strategies shown to extend durability.
  - Cost/environment: Moderate cost; manageable environmental profile at the levels employed.
  - Synthetic feasibility: Straightforward co-precipitation; Co2+ integrates well with Ni/Fe PBA lattices.

- Mn (multivalent stabilizer and activity tuner)
  - Catalytic activity/redox: Mn offers accessible Mn2+/Mn3+/Mn4+ states that can modulate local electron density and oxygen affinity around Ni sites, aiding the dehydrogenation and C–N activation steps that share features with OER-like intermediates in alkaline UOR.
  - Structural role: A-site Mn2+ introduces benign lattice strain and fosters additional defects/vacancies that enhance mass transport and site exposure. It also broadens the distribution of M–N≡C–Fe bond geometries.
  - Stability: Mn’s multivalency buffers charge fluctuations, reducing localized over-oxidation events on Ni; judicious Mn content maintains corrosion resistance.
  - Cost/environment: Abundant and low-cost; benign.
  - Synthetic feasibility: Integrates readily via co-precipitation; commonly used in mixed-metal PBAs.

- Zn (tentatively introduced benign stabilizer)
  - Catalytic activity/redox: Redox-inactive Zn2+ (d10) is not a primary active site but tunes neighboring Ni/Co electronic states by diluting strongly redox-active centers, often sharpening NiOOH transitions and improving turnover stability.
  - Structural role: A-site Zn2+ suppresses Jahn–Teller distortions (unlike Cu2+) and rigidifies the cyanide framework, mitigating lattice collapse and intergranular cracking. Zn helps rationally control [Fe(CN)6] vacancy content, water uptake, and porosity.
  - Stability/corrosion resistance: Zn incorporation is reported to enhance PBA stability and corrosion resistance in alkaline electrolytes by strengthening M–N≡C–Fe linkages and reducing dissolution pathways, a key requirement for long-term UOR in KOH.
  - Cost/environment: Very low cost; environmentally benign—ideal for the stated criteria.
  - Synthetic feasibility: Zn2+ co-precipitates cleanly with other divalent cations in PBA synthesis.

4) High-entropy and multimetal synergy: how it improves UOR
- Electronic structure and redox tuning: Mixing Ni/Co/Mn/Zn at the A-site around [Fe(CN)6] nodes creates a distribution of ligand fields and metal–ligand covalency, which shifts the Ni2+/Ni3+ transition to more favorable potentials and balances adsorption energies for key UOR intermediates. Prior multimetal studies show Co lowers overpotential while Fe increases activity and durability; the high-entropy matrix generalizes and amplifies these effects.
- Defect chemistry and active site exposure: Entropy-driven cation disorder promotes controlled [Fe(CN)6] vacancies and microstrain, generating meso-/macroporosity during activation and exposing more Ni-centered sites, consistent with PBA-derived porous oxides that outperform their dense counterparts.
- Conductivity and mass transport: Co and Fe enhance electronic pathways across M–N≡C–Fe networks; high-entropy-induced microstrain and hydrated channels improve electrolyte penetration and urea diffusion.
- Durability versus NSOR: By stabilizing Ni in an environment where UOR onset is energetically prioritized over self-oxidation, multimetal PBAs mirror the interface/electronic-structure strategy that has been shown to suppress NSOR in advanced Ni catalysts.

5) Synthesis and post-treatment strategies to meet criteria
- One-pot co-precipitation at room temperature: Slowly add K3[Fe(CN)6] to an equimolar aqueous solution of Ni2+/Co2+/Mn2+/Zn2+ salts under vigorous stirring, mildly basic to neutral pH, and with a coordinating polymer (e.g., PVP) to control nucleation/growth. Maintain low supersaturation to ensure homogeneous cation distribution and avoid phase segregation—a key lesson from homogeneous multielement gel/solution syntheses.
- Vacancy and morphology control: Tune metal-to-hexacyanoferrate ratio and ionic strength to set intrinsic [Fe(CN)6] vacancy content (~10–20%), which promotes porosity and water channels. Target nanocubes or nanoframes (100–500 nm) to balance surface area and mechanical robustness.
- Oxyanion functionalization: Brief post-treatment or electrochemical activation in KOH containing borate or phosphate introduces surface oxyanion species that can modulate surface charge and accelerate kinetics, analogous to borate/phosphate promotion reported for multielement Ni–Co–Fe systems.
- Electrochemical activation: Precondition in 1.0 M KOH with urea by cyclic voltammetry to form an ultrathin Ni/Co–oxyhydroxide shell atop the PBA core. This core–shell “self-reconstruction” provides the active oxyhydroxide surface while the multimetal PBA core preserves structural integrity and electronic connectivity.
- Optional mild thermal dehydration (≤200–250°C): Controlled removal of water increases conductivity and porosity without destroying the cyanide framework; avoid higher temperatures that collapse PBAs to oxides unless a PBA-derived oxide route is explicitly desired.

6) Metals to avoid under the stated constraints
- Noble metals (Ru, Ir, Pt, Pd): High cost and resource scarcity despite excellent activity; not aligned with low-cost, environmentally friendly criteria.
- Chromium: Although Cr3+ can form stable cyanide complexes, hexavalent Cr species raise significant environmental concerns; avoid.
- Copper: While Cu can enhance activity in some Ni systems, in alkaline urea/ammonia environments it is prone to complexation and dissolution, compromising long-term stability and corrosion resistance within PBAs.

7) Expected performance and durability (benchmarks and rationale)
- Activity: With Ni as the primary site, and Co to reduce overpotential plus Fe to enhance intrinsic activity, the target onset potential for UOR should be significantly lower than OER in 1.0 M KOH with 0.33–0.5 M urea, aiming for ~1.30–1.35 V vs RHE at 10 mA cm−2 on porous electrodes, consistent with best-in-class non-noble multimetal systems.
- Tafel slope: Expect a reduced Tafel slope relative to binary PBAs (e.g., approaching or below ~30–40 mV dec−1), attributable to improved electronic conductivity and optimized intermediate binding from multimetal synergy.
- Durability: High-entropy mixing, Zn stabilization, and Fe-mediated charge buffering should sustain hours to days of continuous operation with minimal performance decay, with reduced Ni dissolution and suppressed NSOR-driven deactivation.

8) How each criterion is met
- High catalytic activity: Ni foundation; Co reduces overpotential; Fe increases activity/durability; Mn expands redox buffering and defect-enabled activity; high-entropy effects broaden optimal active microenvironments.
- Stability/corrosion resistance: Fe–cyanide backbone; Zn-induced lattice stabilization; controlled vacancies; Co/Fe shift NiOOH to benign potentials; electrochemical pre-activation forms resilient oxyhydroxide shells.
- Cost-effectiveness and environmental friendliness: All selected metals are earth-abundant and low-cost; Zn is explicitly benign; avoidance of noble metals and Cr aligns with environmental and cost constraints.
- Ease of synthesis: Room-temperature co-precipitation with readily available salts and K3[Fe(CN)6]; optional mild post-treatments; scalable aqueous processing.
- Structural features: A-site high-entropy mixing atop a robust [Fe(CN)6] B-site yields a tunable M–N≡C–Fe network with controlled vacancies, microstrain, and hydrated channels that together enhance conductivity and mass transport.

In sum, the (Ni, Co, Mn, Zn)-on-A / Fe-on-B high-entropy PBA directly implements the most effective, low-cost, environmentally aligned design principles validated across multimetal Ni–Co–Fe UOR catalysts and PBA-derived Ni–Fe systems: Ni provides the active UOR platform, Co lowers overpotential, Fe boosts activity and durability through strong framework and redox mediation, Mn contributes multivalent buffering and defect-driven site exposure, and Zn—introduced specifically for its stable valence and benign nature—stabilizes the lattice and enhances corrosion resistance in alkaline KOH. This combination leverages high-entropy effects to optimize electronic structure, defect chemistry, and transport, while remaining simple to synthesize and durable under realistic UOR conditions.
