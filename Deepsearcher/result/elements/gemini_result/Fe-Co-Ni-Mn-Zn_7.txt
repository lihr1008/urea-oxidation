This design integrates the synergistic catalytic power of four active transition metals (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn) with a fifth "stabilizer" element (Zn) to balance activity with structural integrity, cost, and environmental safety.

#### 1. Rationale for Metal Selection

*   **Fe, Co, Ni (The Catalytic Core):**
    *   **Activity:** These three metals are widely recognized as the most active non-precious centers for alkaline oxidation reactions. In the HE-PBA structure, the "cocktail effect" ensures that active sites are distributed randomly, facilitating multi-electron transfer steps required for UOR.
    *   **Mechanism:** Ni and Co act as the primary active sites for urea adsorption and dehydrogenation, often forming oxyhydroxide species (M-OOH) in alkaline media that are crucial for the reaction mechanism. Fe modifies the electronic structure of Ni and Co, optimizing the binding energy of intermediates.

*   **Mn (The Valency Modulator):**
    *   **Activity & Cost:** Manganese is abundant and low-cost. In the context of HE-PBAs, Mn contributes to the high-entropy configuration and possesses multiple oxidation states.
    *   **Synergy:** While Mn-PBAs alone can suffer from Jahn-Teller distortion (leading to instability), its inclusion in a high-entropy lattice stabilizes it. Mn helps broaden the potential window and contributes to the "cocktail effect" by providing diverse redox couples that can facilitate charge transfer during the complex oxidation of urea.

*   **Zn (The Stabilizer & Environmentally Benign Element):**
    *   **Structural Stability:** Unlike the other four metals which are redox-active, Zinc (Zn²⁺) maintains a stable valence state. This is crucial for structural rigidity. In high-entropy lattices, Zn acts as a "pillar" that resists the volume expansion or collapse often caused by the repeated redox cycling of the active metals (Fe/Co/Ni/Mn).
    *   **Corrosion Resistance:** Zn improves the material's resistance to corrosion in harsh alkaline electrolytes (e.g., 1.0 M KOH), preventing the rapid leaching of active metals.
    *   **Environmental & Cost Factors:** Zn is non-toxic (environmentally benign compared to alternatives like Pb or Cr) and extremely cost-effective.
    *   **Unique Role in Synthesis:** As seen in the provided literature (Xu et al.), Zn can be selectively etched. In the **NH₃·H₂O-etching strategy**, Zn sites are preferentially attacked/dissolved, creating rich nanopores and vacancies without destroying the overall skeleton. This increases the specific surface area and exposes more active sites (Fe, Co, Ni, Mn) to the electrolyte.

#### 2. Synthesis and Structural Features

*   **Ease of Synthesis (Co-Precipitation):** The HE-PBA is synthesized via a simple room-temperature co-precipitation method. This scalable approach involves mixing metal salt solutions (containing Fe, Co, Ni, Mn, Zn) with a hexacyanoferrate precursor. This ensures uniform distribution of the five metals at the atomic level, maximizing configurational entropy.
*   **Etching Strategy:** Following synthesis, a chemical etching step using ammonia (NH₃·H₂O) is employed. This process leverages the specific chemical properties of the chosen metals (particularly the amphoteric nature or complexation ability of Zn and Ni/Co) to create a **hierarchical porous structure**.
